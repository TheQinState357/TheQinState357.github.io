A passenger waiting in an airport departure lounge needs to keep her phone charged until her boarding pass is scanned. She requests ten units of energy before boarding. Two nearby devices offer to supply the requested amount wirelessly. On a previous request, each device promised ten units and delivered eight. Given the distance and duration of each transfer, eight units matched the forecast for the first device. The second delivered two units less than predicted. For the passenger’s request, suppose the same allocation yields a forecast of eleven units for each device. If the second device again delivers two units less than predicted, the passenger will receive only nine. We study how evidence from past transfers helps the service coordinator decide which provider to select and how much energy to reserve.
